Integration

Ceremony. Meaning. Embodiment.

Coaching for the season around a ceremony — preparing before, and making sense of it after. Not therapy.

Who this is for

The season around a ceremony.

You have a retreat booked and want to arrive ready. Or you came back three weeks ago and can't explain it to anyone.

Maybe you've been microdosing and something is shifting. Maybe a journey showed you something about your marriage, your work, your faith, and now you have to decide what to do with it. Maybe it was hard, and it hasn't settled.

Most people arrive in the middle. The insight was real. Ordinary life hasn't caught up.

What happens in a session

Sixty minutes, by video. $150.

Before a ceremony: what you're actually going in for. How to prepare. Who's around you, and what the week after looks like.

After: what happened, and what it means. Settling it in your body and your routine. Turning what you saw into what you do — in your relationships, your work, your days.

The experience is not the transformation. What you do with it is.

Most seasons need both. Nothing gets forced into a framework. Nothing you saw gets pathologized.

What this is and isn't

Coaching and education. Stated plainly.

This is coaching and education. It is not psychotherapy, medical care, or treatment. Nothing here provides, encourages, or sources controlled substances.

What happens in ceremony belongs to you and the tradition that holds it. This is the work around it. If what comes up needs clinical care, I'll say so and help you find it.

Where this comes from

Lineage.

I come to this through study and through blood. My ancestry is Taíno, and in that lineage the medicine was always ceremony, never recreation. That shapes how I work: preparation over impulse, reverence over novelty, and the long view. What opens in a night is lived out over years.

A tool for the season

UNFOLD — a 90-day journal.

For the days between sessions. A print journal built to sit beside your practice: daily pages, ceremony pages, practices, the long view. It holds a season. It doesn't prescribe one.
